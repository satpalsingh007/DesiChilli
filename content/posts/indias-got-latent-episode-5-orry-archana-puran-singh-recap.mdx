Samay Raina released episode 5 of India's Got Latent season 2 on YouTube on August 28, with a Netflix release following shortly after. The panel lineup for this episode was one of the more unexpected combinations the show has put together this season: social media personality Orry, veteran actor and *The Kapil Sharma Show* judge Archana Puran Singh, comedian Nishant Suri, and comedian Sharon Verma.

## Why this panel stands out

Part of what makes India's Got Latent work, going back to its first season, is the mismatch it engineers between panelists — pairing someone from mainstream Hindi television with someone whose entire career lives inside internet culture, and letting the contestants' acts filter through both lenses at once. Archana Puran Singh has spent decades as a judge on more conventional variety and comedy formats. Orry, by contrast, built his public profile almost entirely through short-form social content and party-scene visibility, with little formal comedy or judging background before this. Putting the two of them on the same panel, alongside two working stand-up comedians in Suri and Verma, was always going to produce a specific kind of tension — and that contrast is a big part of why this episode has been generating conversation since release.

## What the format still looks like

The mechanics of India's Got Latent haven't changed for season 2, just the scale. Contestants come out to perform an unconventional "latent" talent, and before they do, they're asked to rate their own act — a self-awareness twist that's been part of the show since its first season on YouTube. The panel then responds, usually with a mix of genuine reaction and Raina's trademark deadpan commentary, before scoring the act.

Season 2 has kept the biweekly release rhythm it launched with back in June, alternating between wide-release episodes and members-only bonus episodes that go out first to paying YouTube subscribers before wider circulation.

## The bigger picture for season 2

Episode 5 lands roughly two months into a season that's already had a genuinely strong run by the numbers. The season 2 premiere in June — which featured Alia Bhatt and Sharvari Wagh promoting *Alpha* — reportedly crossed fifteen million YouTube views within hours, and Ormax Media's mid-year report named the show the most-watched Indian streaming title of the first half of 2026, with an estimated audience above 38 million viewers.

That scale is worth keeping in mind against the show's history. Season 1 was pulled entirely by Raina in February 2025, with every episode made private, following backlash over comments made by a podcaster during the season's run. The season 2 relaunch — dual-platform, bigger guest names, tighter production — was clearly built with that episode in mind, and panels like this one (mixing a mainstream TV veteran with an internet-native personality) read as part of that broader recalibration: pulling in a wider, more mainstream audience without losing the show's original unfiltered tone.

## What to watch for

With Orry on the panel for the first time this season, the more interesting question isn't really about any single contestant's act — it's whether a panelist known almost entirely for internet presence rather than comedy or performance critique can hold his own next to three panelists who do this professionally. Early reaction clips suggest the show leaned into that gap rather than smoothing it over, which tracks with how India's Got Latent has generally treated mismatched panels in the past: as the point, not the problem.
