**MrBeast — YouTuber Jimmy Donaldson, the world's most-subscribed individual creator — showed up at a pre-release Ramayana gathering in Mumbai on Saturday, September 26, stood with Ranbir Kapoor and Sai Pallavi, and ended up chanting "Jai Shri Ram" after Ranbir coached him through it. The clip is already doing the promotional work a normal trailer drop often can't: it travelled because it is slightly awkward, slightly warm, and very on-brand for a film that wants the planet, not just the domestic mandir crowd.**

## What actually happened at the event?

The gathering was a special media/pre-release event tied to Nitesh Tiwari's *Ramayana*, held at the DNEG / Prime Focus office in Mumbai. Along with MrBeast, reports put Ranbir Kapoor, Sai Pallavi, Ravie Dubey, producer Namit Malhotra and Tiwari in the room; some coverage also lists Sunny Deol among attendees. MrBeast got a traditional welcome, posed with the cast, and told the room he'd seen part of the film: "It looks phenomenal."

Then someone from the crowd — coverage splits between a paparazzo and a general shout — called "Jai Shri Ram" at MrBeast. He didn't clock what he was supposed to say back. Ranbir stepped in, told him to repeat "Jai Shri Ram," and MrBeast did, to cheers.

A second stretch of the same appearance matters more than the chant if you're trying to read this as anything other than a stunt. Ranbir explained Lord Ram to him, in the version quoted by Free Press Journal: a prince who had everything, sacrificed it to keep his father's promise and his duties, "known as a perfect man," and a hope that the film gives MrBeast "a brief introduction to our culture, to our history and where it all began." That is the line the makers want clipped next to the cheer.

## Why is a US YouTuber at a Ramayana event?

Because this film is not being sold as "another Indian mythological." It is being sold as a global spectacle that happens to be the Ramayana.

The two-part epic has Ranbir as Ram, Sai Pallavi as Sita, Yash as Ravana, and Sunny Deol as Hanuman, with music billed as A.R. Rahman and Hans Zimmer, VFX from DNEG, IMAX/3D, and Sony Pictures on international distribution. Budget talk around the project has sat in the **Rs 4,000 crore** combined-parts neighbourhood — treat that as the widely reported figure, not a studio-audited line item. Release plan: **overseas November 6, India November 8 (Diwali)** — a West-first Friday plus a home-market festival window that already drew questions about who the film is "for."

MrBeast in Mumbai, a day after Taj Mahal videos circulated, is the human version of that strategy. You don't fly the biggest YouTube account on earth to a DNEG office so he can quietly like a teaser. You fly him so a chant, a bow clip, and a "it looks phenomenal" line sit in feeds that do not otherwise open Bollywood trade stories.

## Is this culture, marketing, or both?

Both, and pretending it's only one of those is how these clips get stupid.

The chant is not theology. MrBeast did not arrive as a devotee; he arrived as a guest who needed the line whispered to him. Ranbir's explainer is the actual content: a 20-second Ramayana primer aimed at a guy whose audience is global, young, and not starting from *Bal Kand*. If that makes some viewers bristle — a Hollywood-scale YouTuber being walked through a slogan at a promo — that's a fair discomfort to name. If others read it as a celebrity treating the story as exportable rather than exclusive, that's the film's stated project.

What it is **not**: proof that MrBeast has a new political alignment, or that the film has "won" the West. It is a successful piece of event tape. The film still has to be good in November.

## How does this sit next to the rest of the 2026 promo circus?

Bollywood has spent this month stuffing panels and premieres with people who can move a clip — [Ayushmann on a members-only Latent bonus](/posts/indias-got-latent-s2-bonus-ep-4-ft-ayushmann-khurrana-suhani-shah-kullu-rajat-sood) while Udta Teer is in the air, [Salman using Weekend Ka Vaar as a soapbox](/posts/salman-khan-shirtless-slams-paparazzi-hip-shots). Ramayana is playing a bigger board: if your budget is rumoured in thousands of crores, you need non-India amplifiers, not just another *aarti* photo.

The risk is the same as any culture-as-content moment. The clip will be read as wholesome by the people already excited for Ranbir-as-Ram, and as a costume by people tired of slogans at press junkets. Neither camp is going to be argued out of that in a caption. The only thing that settles it is whether Tiwari's film treats the epic as a story or as a brand activation that happens to have a bow.

Confirmed: Saturday, September 26 Mumbai event; MrBeast on stage with Ranbir and Sai Pallavi; the chant after Ranbir's prompt; MrBeast's on-mic praise for what he saw of the film; Ramayana Part One's November 6 overseas / November 8 India dates as currently reported. Less clear: how deep MrBeast's involvement goes beyond this appearance, whether more cast (Sunny Deol, Yash) were on that specific stage in every account, and whether this becomes a one-off viral or the start of a longer global-creator tour.
