**Nani's The Paradise opened worldwide on September 24 to the biggest Day 1 of his career — roughly Rs 46.25 crore India net and about Rs 77 crore worldwide gross, per Sacnilk — even after critics largely called the Srikanth Odela action drama fragmented, uneven, and short on story. The opening is a star win. Whether the film can hold that money is a different question.**

## How big was The Paradise's opening day, exactly?

Sacnilk's breakdown is the cleanest place to start, because several outlets are quoting the same tracker with slightly different worldwide labels. On the official Day 1 (Thursday, September 24), The Paradise collected Rs 34.65 crore India net across 9,732 shows at about 49% occupancy. Paid previews the night before (Wednesday, September 23) added roughly Rs 11.60 crore net across 900 shows at a much higher ~79% occupancy. Put together, that puts cumulative India net at about Rs 46.25 crore, with India gross around Rs 54.90 crore.

Overseas added roughly Rs 20–22 crore gross depending on the snapshot, pushing worldwide Day 1 gross into the mid-to-high Rs 70 crore range — Moneycontrol cited about Rs 74.90 crore worldwide, while later Sacnilk-facing reports landed closer to Rs 76.90 crore. Treat the exact overseas decimal as still settling; the shape of the story is clear either way: this is a strong Telugu-centric opener with real overseas contribution, not a soft curiosity launch.

Andhra Pradesh and Telangana did most of the domestic heavy lifting. Day 1 state-wise gross tracking put APTG around Rs 31.50 crore, with Karnataka near Rs 4 crore, Tamil Nadu about Rs 1.75 crore, Rest of India around Rs 3.30 crore, and Kerala under half a crore. Language-wise splits reported in trade coverage also underline the point: the Telugu version carried the vast majority of the India net, with Hindi and Tamil adding smaller satellite numbers rather than rewriting the opening.

## How does this compare to Nani's previous openers?

For Nani specifically, this is a new career high. Trade comparisons put The Paradise clearly ahead of HIT: The Third Case — previously his biggest opener, with Day 1 figures in the high-Rs 30 crore worldwide band — and ahead of Dasara, his first collaboration with Srikanth Odela, which opened around Rs 23 crore India net / mid-Rs 30 crore worldwide. Indian Express and India Today both framed The Paradise as nearly doubling Dasara's opening-day India net and more than doubling that film's worldwide Day 1.

That matters because The Paradise was sold, culturally if not in marketing language, as the "bigger" Odela-Nani film: more scale, more action, more Anirudh Ravichander heat, a multi-language release. The opening says audiences showed up for that pitch. It does **not** automatically say the film delivered on it.

Outside Nani's own filmography, the comparison gets less flattering fast. Prabhas' The Raja Saab still sits in a different opening tier — around Rs 54 crore India net and roughly Rs 100 crore worldwide on Day 1 — though that film also ran far more shows. The Paradise can be Nani's biggest without being one of 2026's absolute Telugu-event openers. Both things are true.

## Did the film open this strong *despite* the reviews?

Yes — and that tension is the real Day 1 story. Multiple major reviews landed soft-to-negative on the same day audiences bought tickets. India Today called the film fragmented and short of a substantial story even while praising Nani's commitment. The Hindu labelled it an emotionally distant epic misfire. The News Minute argued the film turns oppression into spectacle and loses the storytelling underneath. 123Telugu landed at 2.75/5, praising Nani and some fight/music stretches while calling the template underwhelming against the pre-release promise.

That split — career-best opening, mixed-to-poor critical reception — usually means one of two weekend paths. Either superfans and brand pull carry Friday–Sunday anyway, buying the film time, or occupancy softens once word of mouth catches the reviews. Occupancy already dropped from the ultra-high preview night into the high-40s on regular Day 1, which is still respectable for a mass action drama of this scale, but not the kind of locked-in frenzy that shrugs off bad notices forever.

## Why did audiences still show up without a trailer?

Part of the opening strength is explained by a deliberate marketing gamble: Nani confirmed before release that The Paradise would have **no theatrical trailer**, arguing the team preferred the film over cutting one more promo under "unavoidable circumstances." He also refused to brand it a pan-India film, keeping Telugu as the "prime target" even with Hindi, Tamil, Kannada, and Malayalam versions in the mix.

Skipping a trailer is unusual for a film this expensive-looking, and it usually either creates mystery or creates confusion. Day 1 suggests the mystery — plus Dasara goodwill, Anirudh's music visibility, and Nani's recent mass-action pivot — was enough to get people into seats once. Holding them through a nearly three-hour runtime (reports put it around 2 hours 54 minutes / 178 minutes, A-certified) after mixed notices is the harder half of that bet.

## What should we watch next at the box office?

The next two days matter more than the opening brag. A career-best Day 1 that collapses on Friday/Saturday still ends as a soft week; a career-best Day 1 that holds 40%+ of that number into the weekend starts looking like a genuine hit trajectory. Given how much of the gross is concentrated in APTG, the more useful early question isn't "can this become a pan-India monster?" — Nani already said that wasn't the framing — but "can Telugu word of mouth keep APTG screens full once the first wave of ticket buyers has spoken?"
