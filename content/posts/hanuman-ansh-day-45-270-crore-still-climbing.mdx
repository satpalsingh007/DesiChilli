**Hanuman Ansh closed its seventh Sunday in theatres, Day 45, with India net collections estimated between roughly Rs 267 and 272 crore depending on the tracker, and a worldwide gross now past Rs 330 crore. Forty-five days into its run, the film is still posting day-over-day growth of 40 percent or more — a pattern that essentially no other release this year has sustained this late into a theatrical run.**

## How much did Hanuman Ansh actually collect on Day 45?

This is a case where being upfront about tracker disagreement matters more than usual. ETV Bharat reported Day 45 collections of Rs 9.51 crore, taking the India net total to Rs 267.64 crore. Sacnilk's independent tracking put the same day at a considerably higher Rs 13.75 crore, pushing its own India net figure to Rs 271.88 crore and worldwide gross to Rs 347.05 crore. A third tracker, TrackTollywood, reported an even higher Rs 17.14 crore for the day, with total gross collection at Rs 333.90 crore. That's an unusually wide spread for a single day's number, and it's worth reading as a signal that final, reconciled figures for Day 45 hadn't fully settled at the time of these reports — treat the roughly Rs 270 crore India net range as the most defensible current estimate rather than any single exact figure.

## How did the film get here week by week?

The full trajectory is worth laying out in full, because the shape of this climb is the actual story, not any single day's number:

- **Week 1:** Rs 1.08 crore total, opening on barely 250 screens
- **Week 2:** Rs 0.40 crore — its quietest stretch, when most films in this position would already be finishing their run
- **Week 3:** Rs 10.40 crore, the first sign of real word-of-mouth taking hold
- **Week 4:** Rs 61 crore, a dramatic jump that turned the film into a genuine trade story
- **Week 5:** Rs 90.25 crore, its strongest week yet
- **Week 6:** Rs 80.25 crore, a slight dip but still far above what a sixth week typically delivers
- **Days 43-45:** Rs 5 crore, Rs 9.75 crore, and roughly Rs 9.5-17 crore respectively, depending on tracker

That week 2 number is worth sitting with for a second. A film earning less than half a crore in its second week is, by almost any normal industry standard, a film about to quietly disappear from screens. Hanuman Ansh instead used that quiet stretch as a launching point for one of the more dramatic box office turnarounds of the year.

## How is this still growing 45 days in?

Most theatrical runs follow a fairly predictable shape: a peak in the first week or two, then a steady, sometimes sharp decline as the initial audience is exhausted. [We've tracked this exact contrast before](/posts/mirzapur-crosses-200-crore-worldwide-7-days) — Mirzapur held unusually steady through its first week, which was itself considered a strong outlier result. Hanuman Ansh isn't just holding steady 45 days in; it posted a 41 percent single-day jump from Day 44 to Day 45 by Sacnilk's count, and a 95 percent jump just two days before that, from Day 43 to Day 44. Those aren't the numbers of a film coasting on residual interest — they're the numbers of a film whose audience is actively still growing, more than six weeks after release.

Same-day comparisons underline how unusual that is. On September 20, Hanuman Ansh's daily collection reportedly outpaced newer releases still in their opening weeks, including [Mirzapur: The Movie](/posts/mirzapur-crosses-200-crore-worldwide-7-days), Vibe, and Daayra — three films with a fraction of Hanuman Ansh's total runtime in theatres, all being out-earned on a single day by a film in its seventh week.

## What does this mean for its place among 2026's biggest films?

Wikipedia's tracked list of 2026's highest-grossing Indian releases currently places Hanuman Ansh among the year's top ten domestic grossers, a genuinely remarkable position for a film that [opened on roughly 250 screens to just Rs 10 lakh](/posts/hanuman-ansh-ott-rejection-60-crore-comeback). Measured purely on return relative to its reported Rs 1.8 crore budget, it remains in a category essentially no other film released this year can approach — [as we noted when it crossed Rs 150 crore](/posts/hanuman-ansh-crosses-150-crore-day-34), that comparison isn't really about which film made the most money, but about which film made the most out of what it actually risked.
