**Bigg Boss 20 voting happens inside the season's official streaming app — expected to be JioHotstar, following the reported digital-first rollout — during a window that opens after nominations air and closes before the weekend eviction episode. You vote to *save* a nominated contestant, not to evict one, and votes cast anywhere other than the official platform or an officially announced route do not count.** That last point is where most viewers lose their votes every season.

## Where does Bigg Boss 20 voting happen?

Voting lives on the show's official digital platform. With this season reportedly launching digital-first on JioHotstar ahead of its Colors TV broadcast, that app is where the vote is expected to sit — the same place the episodes stream.

Two things are worth separating here, because they get conflated constantly:

**The official platform is the only counted channel.** Whatever the show announces on air is the vote. In past seasons this has been the streaming platform's own Bigg Boss page, and some earlier seasons additionally ran missed-call voting on published numbers. Whether a phone-based route returns for season 20 hasn't been stated.

**Everything else is noise.** Google Forms, Instagram story polls, "official" fan sites, YouTube community polls, and Twitter/X polls have no connection to the show's tally. They can be interesting as sentiment readings. They are not votes.

If you take one thing from this page: before you spend any effort voting, confirm from the on-air announcement or the app itself where the vote is. Every other source is a guess about a mechanic the show controls.

## When does the voting window open and close?

The rhythm has been broadly consistent across seasons. Nominations air early in the week, the voting window opens once the nominated names are public, and it closes before the weekend episodes where the eviction is revealed.

What isn't consistent is the exact timing. Windows get shortened, extended, or occasionally frozen entirely when the show runs a twist, and the closing time is often earlier than viewers assume because the eviction episode has to be recorded before it airs. The in-app countdown is the authoritative source — not a schedule someone posted last season.

The practical consequence: voting on the last day is a bad habit. If a window closes early or a twist compresses it, late voters simply don't get counted.

## How do you actually cast a vote?

The flow is short, and it's worth knowing before the first nomination week rather than during it:

1. **Install and sign in.** Vote flows require a logged-in account. Creating one mid-window while a countdown runs is how people miss it.
2. **Open the Bigg Boss page in the app.** Voting is surfaced on the show's own hub, not in a separate section.
3. **Find the active voting card.** It appears only while a window is open. If you can't see it, either the window is closed or nominations haven't aired yet.
4. **Select the contestant you want to save.** Read the interface carefully — see the section below on how many selections you get.
5. **Submit and confirm.** Look for the confirmation state. An unsubmitted selection is not a vote.

## How many votes do you get?

This is the detail that changes most often between seasons, and the one most worth checking rather than assuming. Across the franchise's history, formats have included one vote per account per window, a bundle of votes to distribute across contestants as you like, and separate allocations for subscribers.

Season 20's specific allocation hasn't been announced. When it is, the two things to check are whether votes are per-account or per-day, and whether you can put multiple votes on one contestant or must spread them. Those two rules together decide whether coordinated fan voting or broad casual voting has more influence — which is why fandoms pay close attention to them.

## What are the most common voting mistakes?

**Voting to evict.** The single biggest misunderstanding. There is no evict button. You save someone, and the contestant with the fewest saves is at risk. If you want a particular contestant gone, the only available move is to vote for whoever you'd rather keep.

**Voting for someone who isn't nominated.** Only nominated contestants appear in the window. Support for a safe contestant does nothing that week.

**Assuming last season's rules.** Platform, allocation, and window length have all changed between seasons.

**Voting on an unofficial poll.** Covered above, and it's the most wasted effort in the fandom every year.

**Not checking that it submitted.** Weak connections and expired sessions drop votes silently.

## How do you spot fake Bigg Boss voting sites?

Every season produces a crop of lookalike voting pages, and they're built to catch exactly the search you might be running right now. The reliable tells:

- **It asks for money.** There is no paid vote. Any per-vote charge, "premium vote," or payment to boost a contestant is fraud.
- **It asks for your password.** No voting flow needs your credentials for another service. This is credential harvesting.
- **It isn't the official app or site.** Check the domain against what the show announced. Lookalike domains with small misspellings are the standard trick.
- **It promises unlimited votes.** The whole point of an allocation is that it's limited.
- **It arrived by forward.** WhatsApp forwards and DMs with voting links are the primary distribution channel for these.
- **It wants an OTP.** Never share one. No legitimate vote requires it.

## Why aren't vote counts published?

Bigg Boss has never published running vote tallies, and that's a deliberate format choice rather than an oversight. Live numbers would tell viewers when a contestant is safe, and viewers who believe someone is safe stop voting — which collapses the engagement the mechanic exists to generate. Hidden counts keep every nomination week uncertain.

The side effect is an information vacuum, and unofficial polls rush to fill it.

## How should you read unofficial vote trend polls?

You'll see plenty of "voting trends" content each week, and it's worth understanding what it can and can't tell you. These polls sample whoever happens to see them, which skews heavily toward the most organised fandoms and the most online segment of the audience — while the show's actual voting base includes a large, quieter television audience that doesn't fill in polls.

That's why unofficial trends and real results diverge so often, and usually in the same direction: they overstate contestants with intense niche followings and understate broadly liked ones. Read them as a measure of fan mobilisation, which is genuinely interesting, and not as a prediction.

## Does voting actually decide who leaves?

Mostly, with real caveats. The public vote is the primary mechanism, but a Bigg Boss season has always had other exit routes: contestants have left over rule violations, medical reasons, or personal decisions, and the show has used mid-season twists that override or bypass a standard eviction.

That matters more than usual this season. The reported "Extra Jeevan Daan" mechanic would give evicted contestants a route back into the house through tasks, which means a vote could decide who leaves the main house without deciding who leaves the season.
