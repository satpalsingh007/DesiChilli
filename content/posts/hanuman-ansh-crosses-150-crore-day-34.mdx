**Hanuman Ansh has crossed the Rs 150 crore mark at the Indian box office on Day 34, per Filmibeat's live entertainment tracker, extending a run that's now become one of 2026's clearest examples of a small film building its audience almost entirely through word of mouth.**

## How did a Rs 10 lakh opener get to Rs 150 crore?

The scale of this climb is worth restating plainly, because it doesn't happen often. Hanuman Ansh opened on August 7 to roughly Rs 10-11 lakh on its first day, on a reported production budget of about Rs 1.8 crore. [We covered the film's steady week-by-week climb](/posts/hanuman-ansh-proves-big-budgets-arent-the-point) back when it had crossed roughly Rs 27 crore net, and again when reports put the figure between Rs 60 and 80 crore net closer to Day 27. Crossing Rs 150 crore by Day 34 means the film has roughly doubled its collections in just about a week — a pace that's unusual even by the standards of a film that's already been outperforming expectations for a month straight.

## Why does this milestone matter beyond the number itself?

This isn't a story about one big opening weekend anymore — it's a story about a film that's kept finding new audiences well over a month into its release, which is a much harder thing for any film to do regardless of budget. Most theatrical runs, even successful ones, taper off significantly by their third or fourth week as the initial wave of interested viewers has already been through the door. Hanuman Ansh's trajectory has done close to the opposite, adding tens of crores in a single week at a point in its run where most films are winding down rather than accelerating.

## How does this compare to the year's bigger-budget releases?

Set next to 2026's tentpole releases, the comparison gets genuinely stark. A film with a reported Rs 1.8 crore budget crossing Rs 150 crore net puts its return on investment somewhere in the range of 8,000%, a figure that essentially no big-budget release this year can approach regardless of how large its own total collection ends up being — a Rs 500-800 crore production would need to cross several thousand crore to hit the same ratio. That's not a knock on big-budget filmmaking, which does things a small devotional drama simply isn't built to attempt. But it's a useful reminder that "biggest number on the chart" and "most successful film relative to what it risked" are two different questions, and this run keeps answering the second one more emphatically with every passing week.

## What's still unconfirmed about the final total?

Box office trackers vary in their exact day-by-day figures, and a running total this size is being updated in near-real time rather than confirmed as a final studio-audited number. Treat Rs 150 crore as the latest tracked estimate as of Day 34 specifically — later updates, including from other trackers like Sacnilk or Bollywood Hungama, may land on a slightly different figure for the same date. What's consistent across every tracker so far is the direction: this remains one of the steadiest, longest-sustained climbs of any Hindi release in 2026, regardless of which exact number different sources land on for any single day.
